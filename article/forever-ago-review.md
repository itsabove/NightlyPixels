I wasn’t ready for the emotional rollercoaster that *Forever Ago* became for me. What I thought would be a cute little indie game turned into a journey of feelings, both from the game and my own. It puts you in the shoes of Alfred, who’s on a journey north in search of redemption; along the way, you relive your love with your sweetheart Audrey, capture memories, solve small puzzles, and build friendships on an unforgettable road trip. 

*Forever Ago* might be a walking simulator that, at times, might feel like it’s taking a little too long to get from point A to B, but that didn’t stop me from enjoying this rich story that had me emotionally invested. It evoked my love for my wife and the loss of my mother, but the story is so rich you don’t mind getting sucked into it. 

<h1><b>A Walking Simulator With A Heavy Emphasis On Story</b></h1>

<br>

I’m a big fan of walking simulators; I just don’t play them as often. Games like *What Remains of Edith Finch* and *Life is Strange*, you could say that even the most recent *Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy* fits the mold as well, even tho that last example is more action-filled. They all share a heavy story focus, which is true here as well. Yes, you could say most of the game you’re pushing the joystick forward, but that can’t take away from the tonal changes and the story development that are grasping at your emotions, and doing a great job at it as well. 

<br>

![001](/assets/images/post/forever-ago/FA_02.webp)

<h1><b>Slow Steps And Hidden Barriers</b></h1>

<br>

My main gripe with the game was the slow walking pace. Look, I understand Alfred is an older man, but the pace you walk at made every move to your next objective feel slow at times. I would hit a barrier and have to backtrack a little, which made my time feel a bit more drawn out and, in a way, slightly put me off exploring every edge of this game at the start.

In all fairness, that was about my only complaint with the game, as I found a few other mechanics a bit more fun; the journal was just a push of a button, and it had a quick prompt to show your current objectives, which took away from having to open the journal every time you wanted to check it. This honestly made life easier since I felt like the walking was slow; it prevented me from having to stop and look up the next thing every time I forgot. 

<h1 style="color: #1aa499;"><b>“It felt like a portal to the past.”</b></h1>


As a photography hobbyist, the camera mechanics and usage in this game were fun and easy. I enjoyed taking pictures of certain items on the list for the scrapbook journal, as well as the parts of the story that used it to put you in different times of your life. It felt like a portal to the past, making it enjoyable  to see what Alfred and Audrey were doing in the exact location years before, while also showing some conflict they might have had at that time.

<h1><b>A Beautiful Soundtrack With Emotion</b></h1>

The music in this game is phenomenal. They work alongside Clark Aboud, who composed this emotionally filled soundtrack and elevated it to a new level. People sometimes overlook how much a song can enhance a scene, but it was done with care here. One track that kicks in during a specific part just hits differently. It brought out so much more emotion and nailed the tone, leaving me in awe.


That’s not all; there’s also one of the few licensed songs, "*Nothing Goes Away*" – Performed by Wolfie's Just Fine, that keeps playing in my head over and over. Overall, the music in this game is just a blessing, and a bonus to an already standout story. 

<h1><b>No Need For Side Quest</b></h1>

*Forever Ago* is mostly straightforward with no real side quests; what they do have here is optional side activities and mementos that are woven directly into each stop along Alfred's road trip, rather than tracked as separate side quests. Each area is filled with objectives you can do following Audrey’s latest request, all easy to do and follow along.

<h1><b>Without A Hitch</b></h1>

The game also ran pretty well most of the journey, although at times, even on the Xbox Series X,  it had a section towards the second half of the game where frames dropped only for a little while, but overall nothing took away from the game in a big technical way. Most of the game ran smoothly, letting you immerse yourself in the story without worrying about performance.



<h1><b>Replay It For The Story!</b></h1>

There’s no real reason to start a new game once you complete the story unless you’re trying to complete the trophy/achievment list. Thankfully, if that’s the reason you’re replaying it, they made it pretty easy to clean up anything you may have missed. When the game was released, some were missable, but that was corrected in a later patch that addressed them all and made them completable in chapter select, making it a breeze to clean up.

![001](/assets/images/post/forever-ago/FA_03.webp)

<br>

<h1><b>In Short</b></h1>

<br>

*Forever* Ago is a delightful game with a rich story that holds on to you from beginning to end. If you’re interested in simple puzzles, a touching story, and scrapbooking, I'm sure you’ll enjoy it. To me, this game is a must-play if you’re interested in narrative games.
